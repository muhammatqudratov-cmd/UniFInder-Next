Context: pure CSS change, no logic. Apply the approved gradient B — `linear-gradient(135deg, #15140f 0%, #1c2620 50%, #1a2230 100%)` (black → forest green → navy) — to all five hero sections shown in the screenshots: Home ("Find your campus in South Korea"), Universities ("University Search", already dark — gets the same gradient instead of its current custom dark gradient), Agents ("Meet The Agents"), Community ("Voices On Campus"), and My Page ("Welcome To Your Profile").

These five share one component, `.header-basic` in `scss/pc/main.scss` (rendered by `libs/components/layout/LayoutBasic.tsx`), with light-theme overrides for `&.agent-hero`, `&.community-hero`, `&.mypage-hero` (University has no override, so it already inherits the base dark gradient). The Home hero is a separate component, `.hero-section` in `scss/pc/homepage/homepage.scss` (rendered by `libs/components/layout/LayoutHome.tsx`).

Animation note: the moving colored dots on the Home hero and the floating circle "blobs" on the My Page hero are both driven by JavaScript (a `<canvas>` particle loop in `LayoutHome.tsx`, and CSS `animation: mypageBlobFloat` in `main.scss`) — neither is touched by this change, so they keep running exactly as before. The agent marquee scroll and the glow-circle blur are likewise untouched.

Only background colors and the handful of text colors that sit directly on the hero background (with no own light pill/card behind them) change, so they stay readable on the new dark background. Any text that already has its own light chip/card background (the `.eyebrow.light` pill, the `.accent-chip`, the `marquee-pill`, the `browsing-card`, the `hero-search-card`, the white secondary buttons) is left exactly as-is — those already have correct contrast against their own light background regardless of the page background.
